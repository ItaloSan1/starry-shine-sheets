Genie Z-135/70 · Z13513-1861 · boom angle sensor circuit

Connector card

Where J114, J154 and J20 are, what is on every cavity, and how the six conductors actually run back to the two controllers.

Straight answer about pictures

None of the three Genie manuals contains a photograph of any of these connectors. What they contain is line-drawing parts figures showing where the assembly sits, schematic blocks with cavity numbers, a text connector legend, and cavity-face drawings for the 23-, 35-, 12- and 4-pin connectors — but not for the 6-pin ones.

So: the 12-pin face below is Genie’s own drawing. The photo of J114 is yours, taken at the machine on 12 September — it is the best picture of that connector that exists. The cavity order for the 6-pin connectors comes from the wiring sheet, not from a face drawing, so read the numbers moulded beside the holes rather than counting positions.

Read this off the diagram before you cut anything

Ohm every pair of the six cavities at J114, battery off. The pair that reads about 0 Ω is the loop. 1–6 or 2–5 → somebody was feeding a dead element from the live one: a wiring repair, and the break is in one of the six short jumpers. 3–4 → stop, photograph, do not energise.

J154 secondary boom angle sensor

WhereTurntable riser bulkhead, ground-controls side. Same 6-cavity Deutsch DT, same layout as J114.
Why it matters hereIt hangs off the same 5 V wire and the same ground wire as J114. That is why a fault at the primary can take the secondary down with it — and why measuring J154 with J114 unplugged is worth more than any other single reading on the machine.

J20 12-pin · harness join

What it is“12 pin Deutsch connector lower/upper limit switch harness” — it is the joint between two harnesses: the lower limit switch harness (226496GT on your serial) and the upper limit switch harness (119792GT).
Where to hunt for itItems 6 and 7 on the wire harness diagram, PM p.74 fig 308.1 — where the turntable harness hands off to the boom harness, near the boom pivot end of the turntable. This placement is read off the harness diagram, not stated in words anywhere — treat it as the place to start looking, not gospel.
Why you careAll six primary-sensor conductors pass through it, and the wire colours change across it. Every reading you take at J114 is really a reading through two J20 contacts.

The test this unlocks: unplug J20 and J114 and the six jumpers become an isolated segment. Ring each row of that table end to end — all should be about 0 Ω. The loop at J114 can’t mask a break, because it only bridges within the J114 end. One row reading open is your fault, and it will almost certainly be the mate of whichever cavity the loop feeds.

Reading a Deutsch DT cavity number

The numbers are moulded into the plastic beside each hole, small and unpainted. Hold a torch at a shallow angle across the face and they throw a shadow you can read. Count nothing — on a connector somebody has already been into, position tells you less than the moulding does.

There is no face drawing for the 6-cavity connectors in any of the three manuals, so the order in the tables above is the electrical order off the wiring sheet, not a picture of the physical face. Read the mouldings, then map them to the table.
