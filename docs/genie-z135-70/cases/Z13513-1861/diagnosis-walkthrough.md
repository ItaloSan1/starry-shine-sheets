# Genie Z-135/70 (SN Z13513-1861) — step-by-step electrical diagnosis

**Sources.** SM = Service and Repair Manual 1268557GT (printed page = PDF page − 14). OM = Operator's Manual 114474 (printed = PDF − 2). PM = Parts Manual 106877GT Rev H.04. All PM page and item numbers in this document are from Rev H.04 (July 2024, 358 pages). If your printed copy or the dealer's copy is the older Rev G6 (January 2013) the pages will not match; quote the part number and the section number (e.g. 305.1 Ground controls), which are stable across revisions. ES0366J = the electrical schematic sheet, SM p.229 / PDF 243, titled "Genie Z-135 Electrical Schematic w/ Deutz Engine" and marked "DEUTZ ENGINE SHOWN — SEE OPTIONS PAGES FOR CUMMINS AND PERKINS ENGINES". Engine-side wiring for a Perkins is on "Perkins 1104C-44 and 804D-33T Engine Electrical Schematic" (SM pp.215–216 / PDF 229–230) and for a Cummins on pp.217–218 / PDF 231–232; those pages are drawings only. The sheet is cited below by connector and circuit name only; its grid letters/numbers could not be verified from the manual text and are not used. Every fact below is from those three documents or the repository knowledge base unless marked **[field practice, not Genie]** or **[inference]**.

**Acronyms and words used below.** TCON = turntable controller, the computer in the ground control box. ECM = the TCON's circuit board (Genie calls it the ALC-1000 ECM board; PM 1258461GT); "ECM board" and "TCON board" mean the same thing. DCON = drive chassis controller, the computer down on the chassis that reads the steer and axle sensors. SCON = safety controller, the box on the turntable that holds the six switched safety power outputs (P_38, P_39, P_10, P_11, P_30, P_9B) and the redundant tilt sensors (SM p.203 / PDF 217; p.189 / PDF 203). PCON = platform controller. CAN = Controller Area Network, the two-wire data cable the four computers talk over. Tee = a three-way CAN splice plug (J152, "3 pin SAE Deutsch tee", SM p.207 / PDF 221). LCD = the ground-control screen. E-stop = red Emergency Stop button. VDC = volts DC. WebGPI = Genie's laptop diagnostic tool. OEM = original Genie part, as built. AWG = American Wire Gauge (16 AWG is a common light-duty wire size). SN = serial number. GND = ground (the battery negative side; brown wires). P/N = part number. ESD = static electricity, which can destroy a circuit board; the manual's rule is a grounded wrist strap on the ground screw inside the box or constant contact with grounded machine metal while touching the board (SM p.103 / PDF 117). DTP / AMP / Deutsch / Molex = brands/families of plug; the J-number is the plug label printed on the schematic and in the connector legend (SM pp.205–207 / PDF 219–221). One known misprint: the connector legend (SM p.205 / PDF 219) lists J15 as "Black 4 pin DTP connector on PCON"; the pin legend (SM p.210 / PDF 224, "J15 Turntable Controller") and the sheet ("POWER HARN J15 BLACK 4 PIN" beside the TCON) both place J15 on the TCON, and that is how it is used below. Back-probe = slide the meter probe into the back of a plug alongside the wire while it stays connected. Continuity = a resistance reading near 0 Ω, meaning the two points are joined by an unbroken wire. Ω (ohm) = resistance; "OL" on the meter = open, no connection. LF/RF/LR/RR = left-front / right-front / left-rear / right-rear wheel; the display's blue/yellow pairs are Genie's colour tags: square end = blue, circle end = yellow (OM p.35 / PDF 37), and each end has a blue side and a yellow side (SM p.161 / PDF 175), so "blue/yellow (RF)" = square end, yellow side. EDC = the drive-pump electric displacement control. Slip ring = rotator = swivel: the same part (PM 1253702GT, whose electrical part is the 10-contact swivel 1253696GT) that carries wires between the rotating turntable and the chassis. ES0366J = sheet ES0366 revision J as printed in the Service Manual; the plate's ES0366 is the same drawing number.

## Safety block — do this before anything else

- **The engine is reported to be running. Shut it down now:** push the ground-control red Emergency Stop button in, then turn the key OFF and remove it. Do not restart it, and do not operate any boom, axle or steer function, until Step 3 (the white wire) is finished. A running engine with a live SCON CAN fault means either the faults are stored history or something is feeding the fuel circuit around the SCON safety output P_9B (SM p.189 / PDF 203, "Loss of CAN" row); both have to be settled before the machine is run again.
- Machine on firm, level ground, booms fully stowed, axles as the procedure requires, wheels chocked, turntable rotation lock in, key off and removed, both E-stops in (SM p.171 / PDF 185, "Observe and Obey" configuration list).
- Key OFF and both E-stops IN before unplugging any connector. Remove rings, watches and jewellery (electrocution/burn hazard, SM p.171 / PDF 185).
- **Disconnect the batteries under both turntable covers before service** (OM p.12 / PDF 14; decal 97865 "Disconnect all the batteries before performing service", OM p.5 / PDF 7). Reconnect only for the live-voltage checks listed below, then disconnect again for any resistance (ohm) test.
- **Static (ESD):** "Attach a grounded wrist strap to the ground screw inside the control box … Maintain firm contact with a metal part of the machine that is grounded at all times when handling printed circuit boards OR use a grounded wrist strap" (SM p.103 / PDF 117). On this machine the box floor is rusted through, so the ground screw may not be grounded: prove box-to-chassis ground first (Step 2 ohm test), then use the ground screw for the strap. Batteries disconnected whenever you touch the board.
- Do not raise or extend a boom while the safety envelope is faulted. Never jumper, bridge or "test" past a limit switch, angle sensor, tilt sensor, load switch or SCON output. The calibration toggle switch is a service-mode input, not a bypass.
- Two people are needed for several steps (SM p.171 / PDF 185).
- This machine is after SN 320 (calibration toggle switch at the top of the ground box, SM p.167 / PDF 181) and after SN Z13512-1712 (bypass/recovery key switch on the lower right of the box, SM p.99 / PDF 113; p.168 / PDF 182).

## 1. What the machine is telling us

The LCD wording ("short to 0V", "no response", "zone fault") is not printed verbatim in the manual; the fault table (SM Section 4) is organised by sensor and "Error Type". The matching rows are:

### Group A — six chassis sensors at 0 V (one shared supply)

| Display | Manual row | Page |
|---|---|---|
| Front axle sensor short to 0V | Front Axle Angle Sensor — Value at 0 V | SM p.184 / PDF 198 |
| Rear axle sensor short to 0V | Rear Axle Angle Sensor — Value at 0 V | SM p.185 / PDF 199 |
| Blue/blue (LF) steering sensor short to 0V | Left Front Steer Angle Sensor — Value at 0 V | SM p.186 / PDF 200 |
| Blue/yellow (RF) | Right Front Steer Angle Sensor — Value at 0 V | SM p.186 / PDF 200 |
| Yellow/blue (LR) | Left Rear Steer Angle Sensor — Value at 0 V | SM p.186 / PDF 200 |
| Yellow/yellow (RR) | Right Rear Steer Angle Sensor — Value at 0 V | SM p.187 / PDF 201 |

All six rows carry the same effect, *"Primary up, Secondary up/down and Extend disabled, Alarm sounds"*, and the same recovery: *"Check for 5.0 VDC at the sensor. Check for damaged wiring going to the sensor. Check that the 5.0 VDC LED is lit on the TCON board. Repair or replace as necessary."* (The manual never says where on the board that LED is.)

Plain words: each sensor is a rotary angle sensor with three wires: a 5 V supply, a ground and one signal wire whose voltage (somewhere between 0 and 5 V) tells the DCON the angle. The manuals do not describe the technology inside; the PM says the sensor and its magnet are a matched set (SM p.158 / PDF 172). Reading 0 V on all six at once means the shared 5 V feed is missing or shorted, not six sensors failing together. On ES0366J one wire leaves **DCON J32 pin 20 (P109ANG, green/white, labelled "STEER SNSR PWR (5.0 VDC)")** and feeds the four steer-sensor connectors J107–J110 [drawn on the sheet: P109ANG-GR/WH and P110RT-BK are printed in the steer-sensor cluster beside the "STEER SNSR PWR (5.0 VDC)" / "STEER SNSR GND" pin labels] and, by inference, the two axle-sensor connectors J160/J161 [inference: J32-20 is the only 5 V pin in the DCON pin legend and the axle sensors are read by the DCON; confirm at J160/J161 in Step 9]; the matching ground leaves **J32 pin 19 (P110RT, black, "STEER SNSR GND")** (DCON pin legend SM p.208 / PDF 222). The connector legend (SM p.206 / PDF 220) names them: J107 right rear, J108 left rear, J109 right front, J110 left front (3-pin Deutsch); J160 front axle, J161 rear axle (4-pin Deutsch, SM p.207 / PDF 221). **This 5 V does not cross the slip ring**: the ten swivel contacts on the sheet are labelled "P18, P6R1, C60AXE, DCON PWR, P9A, P12, DCON GND, P7R, CAN HIGH, CAN LOW", and the wire names printed beside them at plugs J33–J38 are P61LSA, P53LS, P21DCON (DCON power), GNDDCON (DCON ground), S56PRV, D82CAN(+), D81CAN(−), C61AXR and C60AXE; the electric swivel is a 10-contact unit (PM p.69, 306.1, item 29A-, 1253696GT "ASSY., ELECTRIC SWIVEL, 10 CON"), and P109ANG is not among the circuits drawn at those connectors. (The knowledge-base playbook 08 said the 5 V came from the TCON through the rotator; the sheet does not show that. The two 5 V supplies share a net name — the pin legends print P109ANG-GR/WH at both TCON J12-26 and DCON J32-20 — but appear to be physically separate **[inference from the pin legends and the sheet; not stated by Genie — nothing in the SM says the DCON generates its own 5 V, and the flattened sheet text cannot prove there is no link between the two]**. The measurement that settles it is comparing J12-26 (Step 4) against J32-20 (Step 6): if one reads 5 V while the other reads 0 V, they are separate.)

**What the six readings prove.** The TCON can only display six sensor readings of 0 V if the DCON is powered and talking on the CAN bus; a dead DCON or a broken rotator CAN leg would show *"DCON CAN Bus — propel disabled"* instead (SM p.176 / PDF 190). So the chassis-side problem is almost certainly the DCON's 5 V output or its harness (Step 6), and Steps 5 and 8 are confirmation, not the first suspect **[inference from the fault table]**.

### Group B — SCON off the CAN bus

*"CAN Bus — Fault Check — Display message on LCD — Check CAN bus wiring from TCON to SCON/PCON. Repair or replace wiring or SCON/PCON."* (SM p.176 / PDF 190.) The related row *"DCON CAN Bus — Disable propel — Check CAN bus wiring from TCON to DCON through rotator"* is the one that would show if the chassis leg were lost.

**SCON Fault Matrix logic (SM p.189 / PDF 203).** The SCON supplies six switched safety power outputs to the TCON; each row of the matrix says which outputs it switches OFF. *"Loss of CAN"* is the only row that turns **all six** OFF: P_38 propel, P_39 turntable rotate, P_10 primary extend, P_11 primary/secondary up, P_30 secondary extend/down and **P_9B ignition/fuel**. That row describes the SCON's own reaction when the SCON loses the bus; it does not say the TCON drops ignition when the TCON cannot hear the SCON.

**The engine-running contradiction.** The P9B row reads: *"Boom has violated the safety limits resulting in the engine being shut off as a safety feature. Use aux to get the boom back into operational limits. Check P54ENG and P58LS between SCON and TCON for damage. Check SCON chart for possible fault conditions."* (SM p.174 / PDF 188.) On the sheet P9B arrives at TCON J12-7 (P58LS, red/black) from SCON J122-10, with its supply side P54ENG (black/white) at TCON J12-4 / SCON J122-9 (pin legends SM pp.209–210 / PDF 223–224; sheet labels "P12-07 P_9B" and "P12-04 P_6R2 P54ENG"). The TCON's ignition/fuel output C21IGN (white) leaves J13-3. On the **Deutz** sheet ES0366J, C21IGN runs to CR28 "ENG FUEL RELAY"; the relay/fuse legend that covers Deutz and Perkins together (SM p.211 / PDF 225) names CR28 "Engine / Fuel Relay" and R21PIGN "Ignition Fuse, 20A, RPM Solenoid". Which CR28 terminal (30/85/86/87/87a) carries what, and what R21PIGN feeds on this machine, is drawn only on the engine schematics: if the engine is the Perkins 804D-33T the draft suspects, confirm the C21IGN routing on SM pp.215–216 / PDF 229–230 (Cummins: pp.217–218 / PDF 231–232) before relying on it. The TCON's internal logic that links P9B-in to C21IGN-out is not drawn on ES0366J; that P9B OFF stops the engine is taken from the P9B fault text ("engine being shut off as a safety feature") and the matrix, not from a traced wire **[inference from manual text, not a drawn circuit]**.

If the engine truly runs while "SCON CAN no response" is displayed, one of three things is true: (1) the faults are stored history, not live; (2) the SCON still sees bus traffic and is holding its outputs up while its own frames are not reaching the TCON — a SCON that has actually lost the bus drops all six outputs, and the TCON would then be expected to show P__ SAFETY SWITCH FAULT messages (rows SM pp.174–175 / PDF 188–189: P3 … P9B on p.174, P10 … P39 on p.175) **[inference]**; the absence of any P__ fault on the displayed list is itself evidence the SCON outputs are up; or (3) something outside the design is feeding the ignition/fuel circuit. Step 7 settles (2) with the manual's own check, P54ENG and P58LS live between SCON and TCON. Item (3) is why the **non-OEM white 16-gauge wire must be traced before any other test**. Note that several OEM wires at the TCON are white: J12-2 P21DCON (DCON/SCON power), J13-3 C21IGN (ignition/fuel), J13-7 C46HRN (horn), J13-10 C30EDC, J11-2 P52PCON, J11-3 C46HN (horn) and J14-25 V04TRL (SM p.210 / PDF 224); C46HRN and C46HN are horn circuits, so a white wire landing there is not an ignition bypass. A wire that lands on C21IGN, CR28 or R21PIGN and comes from anywhere other than where the schematic puts it is an ignition bypass around the P9B safety output and has to come out.

### Group C — boom-angle faults that follow from the above

- *Primary boom angle sensor not calibrated* / *Secondary boom angle sensor not calibrated*: rows "Not calibrated — Primary (Secondary) up only active from TCON, activate alarm — Perform calibration procedure per service manual" (SM pp.177–180 / PDF 191–194).
- *Primary boom angle zone fault*: no row uses "zone". Nearest are "Out of Tolerance — Recalibrate sensor" (SM p.177 / PDF 191) and the matrix rows "Primary Boom angle (crosscheck)" and "Primary Boom safety (max angle)" (SM p.189 / PDF 203). Each boom angle sensor has two independent outputs in one housing (an "operational" channel and a "safety" channel, SM p.204 / PDF 218): the operational one goes to the TCON (J12-32 C123PBS / J12-33 C124SBS), the safety one to the SCON (J122-3 C141PBS / J122-2 C142SBS). With the SCON off the bus the TCON cannot cross-check them. The two sensor connectors are J114 (primary boom, 6-pin Deutsch) and J154 (secondary boom, 6-pin Deutsch) per the connector legend (SM pp.206–207 / PDF 220–221).
- *Secondary boom switch fault*: rows "Secondary Boom Switch Timeout", "Secondary Boom Switches Intermittence Fault (LSS1RS Fault)" and "LSS1RO Fault" (SM p.188 / PDF 202). LSS1RS is the secondary-boom-retracted **safety** switch wired straight into SCON J121-2 (S73SLE, blue/red; SM p.209 / PDF 223). The matrix row "LSS1RS disconnected (SCON pin #2)" (SM p.189 / PDF 203) switches two outputs OFF, but the extracted matrix is a flat column of "OFF" tokens with no column positions, so which two of P_38/P_39/P_10/P_11/P_30/P_9B they are cannot be verified from the text. The only statement in words is the switch description (SM p.203 / PDF 217): LSS1RS is "Used to cut circuits P9B, P_11 and P_30" — three circuits, not two. The knowledge base (04-fault-codes, playbook 08) reads the matrix row as P_11 + P_30 **[M — knowledge-base reading of the column positions, not verified against the printed page]**; read the two OFF cells off the printed matrix before relying on either version. Recovery: *"Check switch for proper operation or damage. Use TCON display menu or laptop with WebGPI to clear faults."*

Bottom line: two independent hardware problems are plausible — a dead DCON 5 V sensor supply, and a broken SCON CAN leg — with the boom-angle messages as consequences. The SCON is mounted on the turntable (PM pp.54–55, 304.1, item 22-); the CAN tees J152A/J152B are printed next to the SCON block in the sheet text, but which harness they belong to is not stated **[inference from label position in the sheet text]**; whether either is in the wet zone below the ground box has to be checked on the machine **[not stated in the manuals]**.

## 2. Order of testing and why

Cheapest and most-common cause first. Each step either clears a whole group or points to the next.

1. Software version and live-vs-history (free; decides which procedures apply).
2. Battery, box ground, connector condition, fuses and the white wire (rust and loose hardware are the most likely single cause; the wire may be masking a safety cut).
3. TCON 5.0 VDC LED and the boom-sensor 5 V — proves the TCON regulator that feeds the **boom** angle sensors.
4. DCON power/ground at J31-2/1 — these do come through the slip ring.
5. DCON 5 V at J32-20/19 — the supply that actually feeds the six faulted sensors. Comparing steps 3 and 5 tells you whether the loss is a TCON problem, a DCON problem, or a chassis-harness short.
6. SCON power J122-1, ground J121-12, CAN J121-6/7, and the P54ENG/P58LS live check.
7. DCON CAN J31-17/18 through the slip ring.
8. Individual sensors only after their supply is proven.
9. Secondary boom switches LSS1RS / LSS1RO.
10. Clear faults, then calibrate. Full sequence (SM p.105 / PDF 119), required in full only if the TCON board or the SCON is replaced: 1 Select engine configuration ("Set engine" in the Unit of Measure and Language menu), 2 Joysticks, 3 Turntable level sensor, 4 Platform level sensor, 5 Axle angle sensors, 6 Steer sensors (Calibrate All Steer Sensors), 7 Secondary boom angle sensor, 8 Primary boom angle sensor, 9 Jib boom bellcrank angle sensor, 10 Select option configuration (Options menu). Start with booms fully stowed and axles retracted (SM p.105 / PDF 119); the individual boom-angle procedures (SM p.83 / PDF 97; p.89 / PDF 103) and the turntable level procedure (SM p.154 / PDF 168, "booms in the fully stowed position and the axles fully extended") then call for axles fully extended. If nothing was replaced, do only the items that show "not calibrated" or whose sensor was disturbed, but keep them in this relative order.

## 3. Numbered steps

**Step 1 — Stop the engine, read the display, then recycle power once.**
*Do:* Push the ground E-stop in (the engine stops). Key to ground controls, pull the ground E-stop out, engine NOT started. Note the software version shown on the LCD when the E-stop is pulled out (SM p.83 / PDF 97). Let the screen scroll and write down every message. Push the E-stop in, wait, pull it out again; note which messages return. With the key on, press Plus and Minus together for the Machine Status screen (SM p.107 / PDF 121). Then key OFF, remove the key, and disconnect the batteries before opening the box.
*Why:* Version decides 6-point vs 2-point calibration (4.01 threshold) and which fault-clearing menu exists (3.11/4.01 vs 3.12/4.02+). Faults that clear on recycle were latched history (playbook 08).
*Expect:* Version such as "VER x.xx"; list of live faults. A live fault re-appears within a few seconds of key-on and cannot be cleared by the Step 11 menu; a history message stays away after the recycle.
*If bad:* If all 11 return, they are live — continue in order; do not start the engine until Step 3 is done. If none of the messages return after the recycle, they were latched history: still do Steps 2 and 3 (rust, hardware and the white wire are pre-operation tag-out items), then Step 11 to clear, then a function test (Section 5). If some return and some do not, work only the groups that returned.

**Step 2 — Battery, TCON power/ground, box ground, connectors and fuses.**
*Do (live part):* Batteries connected, key to ground controls, ground E-stop pulled out, engine NOT started. Meter on TCON J15 (4-pin DTP; TCON pin legend SM p.210 / PDF 224 — note the connector legend, SM p.205 / PDF 219, misprints J15 as being "on PCON"; the pin legend and the sheet's "POWER HARN J15 BLACK 4 PIN" beside the TCON both place it on the TCON): pin 1 B1BAT (red) to pin 2 or 3 GND (brown).
*Do (ohm part):* Batteries disconnected, key OFF. Unplug J15 from the TCON. On the harness plug, meter pin 2 (brown) to the black 3/8-inch standoff on the box mounting bracket (PM p.63, 305.1, item 21, 94982GT "STANDOFF, 3/8" BLACK"; the red 3/8-inch stud item 20, 94981GT, is the power stud): under 0.5 Ω **[field practice, not Genie]**; that this standoff is the box's ground point is inferred from the parts list, not stated **[inference]**. Then, with J15 still unplugged, meter each of the ECM board mounting screws (PM p.63 items 24/25, 12344GT nylock / 49820GT screw — that these fasten the board is inferred from their position in the list next to item 23 ECM PCB; confirm on the 305.1 illustration, PM p.62) to that same standoff: also under 0.5 Ω **[field practice, not Genie]**. Wear the wrist strap on the box ground screw only after this test has passed; batteries stay disconnected while touching the board.
*Do (connectors):* Batteries disconnected. Unplug and inspect every TCON connector J11, J12, J13, J14, J15 and the key-switch Molex J17 (16-pin Molex mini-fit, SM p.205 / PDF 219): look for green or white crust, water, bent or backed-out pins (push each wire gently from the back; a pin that moves is backed out), and rust flakes from the box floor inside the shell. Clean with electrical contact cleaner only; do not scrape plated pins. Replace the box gasket 88177GT (PM p.65, 305.1, item 30) if water got in.
*Do (protection):* Check the ground-box 20 A breaker (PM p.61, 305.1, item 11, 147095GT). On the engine relay/fuse panel (SM p.211 / PDF 225) also check, using the legend's own names: item 6 F23 "Fuse, 30A, Engine / Start / Alternator"; item 11 R21PIGN "Ignition Fuse, 20A, RPM Solenoid" (the fuse the white wire must not be feeding); item 5 CB10 "Circuit Breaker, 20A, RPM Solenoid". The legend gives only those names and ratings. The symptoms usually attached to them — F23: no crank; R21PIGN: engine dies or will not stay running; CB10: no high idle — are **[inference / playbook, not Genie]**. Push any tripped breaker in; a fuse that blows again is a short, not a fuse problem.
*Why:* The 12 V Group 31 battery feeds everything (SM Section 2); a rusted box floor and loose hardware break the board ground path (playbook 08; the ESD ground screw inside the box is at SM p.103 / PDF 117). Genie's own recovery for joystick faults is to "ensure the connector terminals have not backed out" (SM p.172 / PDF 186), and the OM pre-operation list includes "Electrical components, wiring and electrical cables" (OM p.30 / PDF 32). A corroded or backed-out pin at J15 (TCON ground), J12-2 (P21DCON) or J32-19/20 (sensor 5 V/ground) would explain the whole fault list and would be missed by voltage readings taken with the plug seated.
*Expect:* 12.4–12.8 V rested **[field practice, not Genie]**; under 0.5 Ω on grounds **[field practice, not Genie]**; all board screws present and tight; connectors clean, dry, all pins seated.
*If bad:* A high or wandering reading, rust under a screw, or a missing screw/nylock = the board ground path is broken; clean to bright metal, refit all screws, and repeat. Clean and re-fasten before any other test; repeat Step 1.

**Step 3 — Trace and lift the white 16-gauge wire.**
*Do:* Batteries disconnected. Photograph both ends. Identify where each end lands against the TCON pin legend (SM p.210 / PDF 224), the relay panel legend (SM p.211 / PDF 225) and the connector legend (SM pp.205–207 / PDF 219–221). Check with ohms whether it connects to J13-3 C21IGN, a CR28 socket terminal (30/85/86/87/87a — which terminal does what is drawn only on the engine sheets), R21PIGN, J12-7 P58LS, J12-4 P54ENG or J12-2 P21DCON. Then lift the wire at both ends, label and insulate the ends, before any live test in Steps 4–9.
*Why:* See the P9B discussion above. "If damage or any unauthorized variation from factory delivered condition is discovered, the machine must be tagged and removed from service" (OM p.29 / PDF 31); the pre-operation checklist names "unauthorized modifications" and "Excessive rust, corrosion or oxidation" (OM p.30 / PDF 32). Leaving a parallel feed on P21DCON during the live tests would make it impossible to confirm the OEM feed in Step 5, and P21DCON is also the SCON's supply (J122-1, SM p.209 / PDF 223): if the white wire brings unswitched battery to that net, the safety controller and DCON stay powered independently of the TCON/E-stop path.
*Expect:* Several OEM wires in this box are white (J12-2 P21DCON, J13-3 C21IGN, J13-7 C46HRN, J13-10 C30EDC, J11-2 P52PCON, J11-3 C46HN, J14-25 V04TRL); the schematic does not print wire gauge. Identify the added wire only by where each end lands and by the fact that it is not in the pin legend for that pin, never by colour or thickness. Expect one end on a TCON pin, relay socket or fuse and the other end somewhere it does not belong.
*If bad:* If it feeds the ignition/fuel circuit, it is an ignition bypass around the SCON safety output; it stays out, and the engine must only run through P9B. If it feeds P21DCON, someone was already chasing DCON/SCON power: it still stays out; Step 5 then measures P21DCON at TCON J12-2, DCON J31-2 and SCON J122-1 with only the OEM feed present. Reinstate nothing that is not on ES0366J (or the applicable engine sheet).

**Step 4 — TCON 5.0 VDC LED and the boom-sensor 5 V.**
*Do:* Batteries connected, key to ground controls, ground E-stop pulled out, engine NOT started. Look (do not touch the board; wrist strap per the Safety block) for a lit 5.0 VDC LED on the ECM board. Then measure the 5 V at the sensor end, the way the manual does for the axle and steer sensors (SM p.161 / PDF 175 step 7; p.167 / PDF 181 step 3): back-probe the supply and ground pins of the primary sensor connector J114 and the secondary sensor connector J154 (6-pin Deutsch, SM pp.206–207 / PDF 220–221). Optional **[field practice, not Genie]**: back-probe TCON J12 (black 35-pin) pin 26 P109ANG (green/white, "SNSR PWR") to pin 25 SNSR GND (brown) — J12-25 and J12-26 are adjacent pins on a 35-way AMP, and a slipped probe shorts the TCON's 5 V regulator, so prefer the sensor-end measurement.
*Why:* "Check that the 5.0 VDC LED is lit on the TCON board" and "Check for 5.0 VDC at the sensor" are the recovery text on every angle-sensor "Value at 0 V" row (boom, axle and steer sensors, SM pp.177–180 and 184–187 / PDF 191–194 and 198–201). The joystick rows and the turntable/platform level-sensor rows in the same page range also have "Value at 0 V" entries, but their recovery text is different (joysticks: check wiring and connectors, substitute a known good joystick, or "Power up controller with problem corrected"; level sensors: "Check that SCON is grounded" / "Replace SCON"), so the LED check applies only to the boom, axle and steer angle-sensor rows (SM pp.177–180 and 184–187 / PDF 191–194 and 198–201); the jib angle sensor row (SM p.188 / PDF 202) has different recovery text ("Power up controller with problem corrected"). On the sheet J12-26 (SNSR PWR) is the TCON's sensor supply; the boom-angle sensors are the sensors on that supply, not the chassis sensors.
*Expect:* LED lit; 5.0 V.
*If bad:* Key OFF, E-stops in, batteries disconnected; unplug J154, then J114. Reconnect the batteries, key to ground, E-stop out, and re-measure after each. If 5 V returns, one boom sensor or its harness is shorted (PM p.169, 511.2, item 10; PM p.141, 502.2, item 29). Still 0 V with both unplugged → TCON board (PM p.63, 305.1, item 23-, 1258461GT).

**Step 5 — DCON power and ground.**
*Do:* Batteries connected, key to ground controls, ground E-stop pulled out, engine NOT started. DCON J31 (black 23-pin AMP, SM p.208 / PDF 222; DCON location PM pp.36–37, 205.1, item 18): pin 2 P21DCON (white) to pin 1 GND-DCON (brown). Before that, with batteries disconnected, unplug and inspect DCON J31 and J32 for crust, water, bent or backed-out pins.
*Why:* P21DCON and GNDDCON are two of the circuits that cross the slip ring: the sheet labels the swivel contacts "DCON PWR" and "DCON GND" between the two limit-switch-harness plugs on the turntable side (J34/J35) and the drive-chassis-harness plugs on the chassis side (J37/J38). The harness name of each plug is verified (J33 boom harness; J34/J35 limit-switch harness; J36/J37/J38 drive-chassis harness), but which limit-switch plug carries DCON PWR and which carries DCON GND, and which chassis plug pairs with which, cannot be read from the manual text, and none of J33–J38 appears in the connector legend (SM pp.205–207 / PDF 219–221) **[plug pairing inferred from the sheet text]**. So identify the plug by the white P21DCON / brown GNDDCON wire, not by the J-number. The contact pin numbers cannot be read from the manual text either, so identify the contact by wire colour and circuit name, not by a pin number. P21DCON starts at TCON J12-2 (sheet label "DCON ECU PWR P12-02") and also feeds SCON J122-1.
*Expect:* Battery voltage; J31/J32 clean, dry, all pins seated.
*If bad:* Measure P21DCON at TCON J12-2, then on the turntable side of the swivel (the white "DCON PWR" wire at one of the two limit-switch-harness plugs J34/J35) and on the chassis side (the white wire at one of the drive-chassis-harness plugs J37/J38); same for GNDDCON (the brown "DCON GND" wire at the other limit-switch plug and the other chassis plug) **[plug pairing inferred from the sheet text]**. Voltage on one side only → rotator (PM p.69, 306.1, item 29A, 1253702GT). Do not rotate the turntable under power at this stage (see Step 8).

**Step 6 — DCON 5 V sensor supply.**
*Do:* Batteries connected, key to ground controls, ground E-stop pulled out, engine NOT started. DCON J32 (white 23-pin AMP): pin 20 P109ANG (green/white) to pin 19 P110RT (black).
*Why:* This single output feeds the four steer sensors [drawn] and, by inference, the two axle sensors (Step 9 confirms at their connectors). Sheet label: "STEER SNSR PWR (5.0 VDC)" / "STEER SNSR GND". Six sensors reading 0 V at the same time is a supply problem; the DCON must be alive and talking on CAN for the TCON to show these readings at all (a dead DCON would show "DCON CAN Bus" instead, SM p.176 / PDF 190), which is why this step comes before replacing anything.
*Expect:* 5.0 V.
*If bad:* 0 V with Step 5 good → key OFF, E-stops in, batteries disconnected; unplug J110, J109, J108, J107 (PM p.29, 203.1, item 16) and J160, J161 (PM p.33, 204.1, item 19) one at a time; after each, reconnect the batteries, key to ground, E-stop out, and re-measure. 5 V returns → that sensor or its branch of the drive chassis harness (PM p.75, 308.1, item 8, 94956GT) is shorted to ground. Still 0 V with all six sensors unplugged → key OFF, batteries disconnected, unplug J32 from the DCON. Ohms on the harness plug between pin 20 (P109ANG) and pin 19 (P110RT), and between pin 20 and chassis ground: both must read open (OL / many kilo-ohms) **[field practice, not Genie]**. Near 0 Ω = the harness 94956GT is shorted; find the chafe or replace it. Open → reconnect the batteries, key ON, E-stop out, and measure 5 V directly at DCON header pin 20 with J32 still unplugged (or with J32 plugged back in and nothing else connected) **[field practice, not Genie]**; no 5 V there → DCON 101004GT. If Step 6 is *good* → the faults are history, or the break is downstream (Step 9).

**Step 7 — SCON power, ground, CAN and the P54ENG/P58LS check.**
*Do (live):* Batteries connected, key to ground controls, ground E-stop pulled out, engine NOT started. SCON (PM pp.54–55, 304.1, item 22-). J122 (black 12-pin, SM p.209 / PDF 223): pin 1 P21DCON (white) to J121 pin 12 GNDSCON (brown). Then the manual's own check for the engine question: P54ENG (black/white) at SCON J122-9 (from TCON J12-4; sheet label P_6R2) and P58LS (red/black) at SCON J122-10 / TCON J12-7 (sheet label P_9B), each to GNDSCON.
*Do (ohms):* Key OFF, E-stops in, batteries disconnected. Ohms J121-12 to TCON J12-1 (the two ends of the same GNDSCON wire). Then continuity J121-6 D82CAN(+) (yellow) to TCON J11-18 and J121-7 D81CAN(−) (green) to TCON J11-17, through the CAN tee(s) J152A/J152B (J152 is listed only as "3 pin SAE Deutsch tee", SM p.207 / PDF 221; which of the two tees sits in the SCON leg is not identified in the manual — the sheet text simply prints J152A and J152B near the SCON block with pin letters AA/AB/AC/BA/BB/BC). Unplug and inspect J121 (grey) and J122 (black) and the CAN tees J152A/J152B for corrosion, water and backed-out terminals.
*Why:* "Check that SCON is grounded" is the manual's own recovery for tilt-sensor faults (SM p.180 / PDF 194). The CAN Bus row says check wiring TCON to SCON/PCON (SM p.176 / PDF 190). The P9B row says "Check P54ENG and P58LS between SCON and TCON for damage" (SM p.174 / PDF 188). The lower limit-switch/SCON harness is 226496GT (PM p.75, 308.1, item 6-).
*Expect:* Battery voltage at J122-1; near 0 Ω on ground and on each CAN wire; connectors clean, dry, all pins seated. P54ENG and P58LS both at battery volts with the key on and E-stop out means the P_9B path from TCON through the SCON and back is complete, i.e. the SCON is not in its Loss-of-CAN state **[expected values are field practice; the manual asks only for a damage check]**. P54ENG present and P58LS at 0 V means the SCON is holding P_9B off — check the SCON chart (SM p.189 / PDF 203) and stop looking for a wiring bypass until this is understood.
*If bad:* Repair the harness or connector. Only if power, ground and both CAN wires are proven good and the fault stays live is the SCON itself suspect (1258463GT; replacement requires full machine calibration in the sequence of Section 2 item 10; the knowledge base records that the SCON cannot be field-calibrated).
*Optional:* **[field practice, not Genie]** with batteries disconnected and all modules plugged in, about 60 Ω between CAN(+) and CAN(−) at any node; about 2.5 V each to ground at idle with power on. No resistor in the sheet text is labelled as a CAN terminator. The resistors that are drawn — three "330 OHM" beside P_39/P_7R/P_10, an "82 OHM PULL UP" and a "100 OHM PULL UP" beside P_6R2/P11 and the "5 VOLT" labels, and the "RESISTORS ADDED FROM S/N 1225 TO S/N 1418" beside P21DCON/C61AXR, which are the PM's resistor assemblies T108693GT "P7R/P6R1" and T108692GT "P6R2" (PM p.75, 308.1, items 1-/6-, from SN 1225 to 1418, so not fitted to SN 1861) — all sit beside safety-power circuit labels in the sheet text, not beside the CAN pair. Whether terminators are inside the end modules cannot be read from the text **[inference]**.

**Step 8 — DCON CAN through the swivel (static test).**
*Do:* Key OFF, E-stops in, batteries disconnected, turntable rotation lock in (SM p.171 / PDF 185). Unplug and inspect the swivel plugs J33–J38 for crust, water and backed-out pins. Continuity of D82CAN(+) yellow and D81CAN(−) green from DCON J31-18/17 through the chassis-side plug J36 (drive-chassis harness) → slip ring → turntable-side plug J33 (boom harness) to TCON J11-18/17 (pin legends SM pp.208 and 210 / PDF 222 and 224). The J33↔J36 pairing is read from the order of labels in the sheet text and the matching A/B pin pattern printed at J33 and J36; none of J33–J38 is in the connector legend (SM pp.205–207 / PDF 219–221), so identify the plug by the yellow/green CAN wires, not by the J-number **[plug pairing inferred from the sheet text]**. Identify the swivel contact by wire colour and circuit name, not by a pin letter: the manual text at J36 pairs the yellow "CAN HIGH" wire with contact B and the green "CAN LOW" with contact A, the reverse of what an earlier draft assumed, and the flattened text is not conclusive, so read the letters off the printed sheet before writing them into a report. A swapped pair would give a false "open".
*Why:* "Check CAN bus wiring from TCON to DCON through rotator" (SM p.176 / PDF 190).
*Expect:* Near 0 Ω on each wire; plugs clean, dry, all pins seated.
*If bad:* Rotator 1253702GT (PM p.69: previous swivels 122918, 89437 and 107533 "are no longer available").
*Optional powered test:* **[field practice, not Genie]** — only after Step 7 has proven the SCON on the bus with no P__ faults, booms stowed, axles as required, from ground controls, within the permitted rotation zone, with a spotter: rotate the turntable slowly while a helper watches the LCD for a "DCON CAN Bus" message or the axle/steer readings dropping out. A turntable of this weight cannot be turned without hydraulic power, so this cannot be combined with the batteries-off ohm test; and with the axles retracted LST1S stops rotation past either rear tyre (SM p.203 / PDF 217), so the test cannot sweep the whole slip ring.

**Step 9 — Individual sensors (only after Step 6 shows 5 V).**
*Do:* Steer sensors: batteries connected, key to ground controls, ground E-stop pulled out, engine NOT started (SM p.161 / PDF 175 step 5). Axle sensors: key to platform controls, red E-stop pulled out at both ground and platform controls (SM p.167 / PDF 181 step 1). First, batteries disconnected, unplug and inspect J107–J110, J160 and J161 for crust, water and backed-out pins. Steer sensors J107 (RR), J108 (LR), J109 (RF), J110 (LF) (connector legend SM p.206 / PDF 220), 3-pin Deutsch: the manual probes pins B and C for the signal (SM p.161 / PDF 175 step 7), so B (blue) to C (black) = signal and A (red) to C = supply, the supply pin being the remaining one on a 3-pin plug **[inference]**. Axle sensors J160 (front), J161 (rear), 4-pin Deutsch: the manual probes pins 2 and 3 (SM p.167 / PDF 181 step 3), so 2 (blue) to 3 (black) = signal and 1 (red) to 3 = supply **[inference for pin 1]**.
*Why:* "Check for 5.0 VDC at the sensor" (SM pp.184–187 / PDF 198–201).
*Expect:* 5.0 V supply. Signal, wheels straight and axles retracted: LF (square end, blue side) and RR (circle end, yellow side) **1.4–1.6 V**, RF (square end, yellow side) and LR (circle end, blue side) **3.4–3.6 V** (SM p.161 / PDF 175); axle sensors **4.2–4.4 V** (SM p.167 / PDF 181). Smooth change as the wheel or axle moves. Connectors clean, dry, all pins seated.
*If bad:* No supply at a connector with 5 V at J32-20 → drive chassis harness 94956GT. Supply present, signal 0 or stuck → sensor: steer sub-assembly 106187GT (SM p.158 / PDF 172: "both the sensor and magnet must be replaced as a set"), axle 94985GT; check the actuator pin 60618GT. Any replaced or rotated sensor must be calibrated (Step 12).

**Step 10 — Secondary boom switches.**
*Do:* Booms stowed, key OFF, E-stops in, batteries disconnected. Inspect LSS1RS (J46, 4-pin Deutsch; PM p.139, 502.2, item 15, 110910GT) and LSS1RO (J49; item 12, 110909GT) for damage, roller contact and loose wiring; check continuity of S73SLE (blue/red) from J46 to SCON J121-2. Before testing LSS1RS at the switch, identify on the sheet which J46 pins carry S73SLE: ES0366J draws LSS1RS with a NO contact pair (13–14) and an NC contact pair (21–22) on four wires (BK/BR/RD/WH).
*Why:* Recovery text SM p.188 / PDF 202; LSS1RS is the safety back-up that cuts P9B, P_11, P_30 (SM p.203 / PDF 217).
*Expect:* LSS1RO is normally open, held closed (NOHC) by the fully retracted secondary boom (SM p.203 / PDF 217); the PM lists its element as a NO/NO roller switch (item 12-, 110771-64463GT). LSS1RS is described only as "Backup switch for LSS1RO" (SM p.203 / PDF 217); its element is NO/NC (item 15-, 110771-44463GT), so with the boom retracted the NO pair is held closed and the NC pair is held open, and only one pair is "closed" at a time **[contact arrangement from the PM and the sheet; not spelled out by Genie in words]**. Meter check at the unplugged switch connector: ohms across the held pair with the boom retracted = near 0 Ω; press the roller/plunger by hand and that pair goes open (OL) while the other pair does the opposite. A switch that is open on its held pair while the boom is retracted, or that flickers when the wire is wiggled, is the fault. Verify by the displayed effect after clearing, not by assuming a single contact.
*If bad:* Replace the switch; do not shim or bridge it.

**Step 11 — Clear faults.**
*Do:* With the key switch OFF, press and hold the Enter button and turn the key switch on; release Enter (SM p.107 / PDF 121). Software 3.11 or lower and 4.01: (minus)(minus)(previous)(previous) → Delete Faults. Software 3.12 and 4.02+: (minus)(previous)(previous)(minus) → Clear all safety switch faults (SM pp.107–108 / PDF 121–122).
*Why:* Resets active latching faults; "will not clear fault history". Do not use "Delete all" without Genie Product Support (SM p.108 / PDF 122).
*Expect:* Group A and B messages gone; only "not calibrated" may remain.
*If bad:* A fault that returns immediately is still live — go back to the step that covers it.

**Step 12 — Calibrate in the manual's sequence.**
*Do:* Follow the full sequence in Section 2 item 10 (SM p.105 / PDF 119); the manual requires the whole sequence when the ALC-1000 board (TCON) or the SCON has been replaced, and warns that calibrating out of sequence "could cause the machine to tip over". Only the applicable items are performed here:
- Select engine configuration and Joysticks: only if the TCON board was replaced. Note the "Set engine" menu prints only two choices, Deutz TD2011L04i (DL04i) and Perkins 1104D-44T (P1104) (SM p.107 / PDF 121); see open question 1.
- Turntable level (SM pp.154–157 / PDF 168–171; firm level ground, booms fully stowed, **axles fully extended** — SM p.154 / PDF 168 note): **only if the SCON or the TCON board was replaced.**
- Axle angle sensors (SM pp.167–169 / PDF 181–183, axles retracted, boom stowed, two people): required if a sensor was removed, replaced or its cover rotated in Step 9.
- Steer sensors: a replaced sensor uses the **mechanical** procedure "How to Calibrate a Replacement Steer Sensor" (SM p.160 / PDF 174) — engine running from the platform controls, correct steer mode selected, a second person on the foot switch, sensor cover rotated until the tyre is straight; no calibration mode and no bypass key. "Calibrate All Steer Sensors" (SM pp.161–163 / PDF 175–177) is only for a replaced TCON and starts with key to ground and E-stop out (step 5) and the voltmeter adjustment (steps 6–8) before the bypass key (step 9).
- Secondary then primary boom angle (SM pp.89–94 / PDF 103–108 and pp.82–87 / PDF 96–101; firm level ground, booms stowed, drive-enable zone, **axles fully extended**): required — both show "not calibrated". Before 4.01 use the 6-point method with the digital level kit 58351; 4.01+ use 2-point.
- Jib bellcrank: only if disturbed.
- Select option configuration: only if the TCON board was replaced.

Entry for the display-driven procedures (boom angle, axle, turntable level, all-steer): push the ground-controls E-stop in; do not turn the key off; turn the key to ground controls (SM p.83 / PDF 97 steps 1–2; axle procedure step 6, p.167 / PDF 181). Calibration toggle at the top of the box moved left, a door fastener fitted so the door cannot move it, main key removed and put in the bypass/recovery switch turned to BYPASS ("values will not be saved correctly unless the key switch is in the bypass position and the calibration toggle switch is activated"). Then hold Enter about 5 s while pulling the ground-controls E-stop out (primary: p.84 / PDF 98 step 9; secondary: p.90 / PDF 104 step 9; axle: p.168 / PDF 182 step 13), then (plus)(enter)(enter)(plus). (The platform level sensor procedure instead holds Enter while turning the key to ground controls, SM p.42 / PDF 56 — not needed here.)

Saving: pressing and HOLDING the engine start button for about 5 s until the engine stops is what saves the values (axle: step 21, SM p.169 / PDF 183; primary 6-point: step 27, p.85 / PDF 99; primary 2-point: step 55, p.87 / PDF 101; secondary 6-point: step 26, p.92 / PDF 106; secondary 2-point: step 51, p.94 / PDF 108). Follow the exact order printed in the procedure you are in: the boom-angle procedures do the engine-start hold and then scroll to EXIT, Plus for YES, Enter; the axle procedure scrolls to EXIT, YES, Enter and then does the engine-start hold. "Do not turn the engine off with the key switch or red Emergency Stop button or all calibration points or values will not be saved." Then "Wait approximately 20 seconds and turn the machine off by pressing the red Emergency Stop button in", return the key to RUN and to the main switch, close the box (the toggle resets when the door closes) and remove the door fastener.
*Why:* "Failure to properly calibrate the machine could cause the machine to tip over" (SM p.83 / PDF 97). The manual restricts calibration to Genie-trained technicians (SM p.105 / PDF 119).
*Expect:* No calibration faults after restart ("Be sure there are no calibration faults", SM p.86 / PDF 100).
*If bad:* Repeat; "If the system exits out of calibration mode when the engine is started, repeat step 13" (the Enter-hold step; SM p.168 / PDF 182).

## 5. Before it goes back to work

1. Recycle power; confirm the LCD "display no error messages" (OM p.31 / PDF 33).
2. Clear latched faults with the menu for your software (Step 11). History stays; that is normal.
3. Complete calibrations in sequence (Step 12); confirm "no calibration faults" after restart (SM p.86 / PDF 100).
4. Pre-operation inspection (OM p.30 / PDF 32): no excessive rust, no loose fasteners, no unauthorized modifications — the box floor, hardware and white wire must all be resolved (tag-out rule OM p.29 / PDF 31).
5. Full function tests at ground and platform controls (OM pp.31–38 / PDF 33–40), including Test the Tilt Sensor, the PRI BOOM ANGLE TO GRAVITY / SEC BOOM ANGLE readouts and secondary raise-then-extend / retract-then-lower sequencing (Test the Operating Envelope, OM p.34 / PDF 36 steps 33–34), all four steer modes (Test the Steering, OM pp.35–36 / PDF 37–38), Test the Drive Enable System and Test Limited Drive Speed (OM pp.37–38 / PDF 39–40).
6. Test the Primary and Secondary Boom Angle Sensor and Test the Level Sensor per the Genie Maintenance Manual (referenced at SM p.86 / PDF 100 and p.157 / PDF 171; not in the three source documents).
7. Annual inspection by a qualified person (decal 52865; OM p.29 / PDF 31 requires repairs by a qualified service technician).

## 6. Open questions for the owner

1. Which engine is fitted? The Parts Manual index (PM p.7) gives the engine breaks: Cummins B4.5L to SN 950 (401.1), B3.3T from SN 951 (401.2/402.2); Deutz BF4L 2011 / TD2011L04i with no break (403.1/404.1); Perkins 1104C-44 to SN 952 (405.1/406.1), 804D-33T from SN 953 (405.2/406.2). The SM engine specifications (pp.7–17 / PDF 21–31) give 804D-33T 83 hp / 62 kW (gross); 1104C-44 83 hp / 62 kW net (86 hp / 64 kW gross); B3.3T and B4.5L 80 hp / 60 kW; TD2011L04i 74 hp / 55 kW; BF4L 2011 69–73 hp / 51–54 kW. Of the engines listed for SN 1861, only the 804D-33T is 62 kW, which matches the plate **[inference from the PM breaks and SM power ratings]**. It fixes the engine harness (PM p.75, 308.1, item 5-, 94955GT "HARNESS, ENGINE, PERKINS J3") and which engine schematic to use for the C21IGN/CR28 routing (SM pp.215–216 / PDF 229–230). Note the SM's "Set engine" menu (p.107 / PDF 121) lists only DL04i (Deutz TD2011L04i) and P1104 (Perkins 1104): if your engine is the 804D-33T, ask Genie Product Support which selection applies before running any Full Machine Calibration.
2. Software version shown at E-stop pull-out — decides 2-point vs 6-point calibration and which fault-clearing menu exists.
3. Are the 11 faults live after a power recycle, or stored history? A running engine with a live "SCON CAN no response" and no P__ fault is what Step 7's P54ENG/P58LS check is for.
4. Where does each end of the white 16-gauge wire land? Photos of both ends, please.
5. Has anyone loosened or rotated a steer or axle sensor cover, or replaced any sensor, since the faults appeared?
6. Was a "DCON CAN Bus" message or any "P__ SAFETY SWITCH FAULT" ever displayed alongside these?
7. Is the SCON or either CAN tee J152A/J152B physically in the wet area below the ground control box on this machine?
